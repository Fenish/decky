# 2026-09-09

## Touch replaces the switch matrix, and it worked first time

The user dropped the 60 tact switches and the MCP23017 in favour of the panel's
own GT911, with the plexi keycaps and key carriers going too - the finger
touches the glass directly now.

The notes said this was impossible, or at least expensive: touch was recorded as
*permanently* out of scope, with a PCA9557 boot sequence standing between the
firmware and the controller. That was wrong, and finding out cost one grep. The
pinned LovyanGFX ships `LGFX_Elecrow_ESP32_Display_WZ8048C070.h`, whose twenty
RGB pins match `panel.cpp` exactly - so it describes this board, not a cousin -
and its touch block wires the GT911 with `pin_rst = -1`, `pin_int = -1` and no
expander at all. LovyanGFX also already had a GT911 driver compiled into the
build, which tries both possible I2C addresses by itself.

It came up on the first flash: `touch: device at 0x14 <- GT911`, `GT911 ready`.

Worth keeping: the PCA9557 warning was an *Advance* model caveat, and the notes
had already made exactly that mistake once before about the SD card's DIP
switches. Two for two. An Elecrow caveat should be checked against the model
before it is believed.

There is a real PCA9557 on the bus - a second device answers at 0x18 - it is
just not in the way.

## What the board now runs

Fifteen numbered keys over two pages. Bottom right pages forward, bottom left
pages back, numbers run 1 to 27 straight through so a page change is visible
rather than inferred. Presses light the key and print to serial.

The GIF player, SD storage and media push are no longer built, but nothing was
deleted - the libraries are still in the tree and simply unreferenced, so
PlatformIO skips them. The image dropped from 769 KB to 443 KB and boot no
longer waits on the card. The `ID` handshake is kept so the desktop app still
finds the deck.

## Two traps found while flashing

`git init` happened here first, at the user's suggestion, before any of the
rewrite - the repo had no version control at all until today.

The flash appeared to succeed while telling us nothing: esptool's progress bar
killed PlatformIO with a cp1252 `UnicodeEncodeError`, and PlatformIO printed
`[SUCCESS]` anyway. `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8` fixes it, and `Hash of data
verified` is the line that actually confirms a write. Also, the board was on
COM5, not the COM4 the notes named.

## Press feedback tore, and the page draw was worse

The user reported the press highlight "sometimes glitching". It was tearing:
`draw_key()` wrote straight into the buffer being scanned out, with no beam
check, because I judged a single key flash too brief to see. Measured, that
judgement was wrong - a key takes 1.65 ms to write and the beam crosses one in
4.8 ms, so roughly a third of presses collide. The repo had already solved this
once; `beam_clear_of()` came back out of the first commit.

Measuring to size the estimate found a second defect I had not been told about
and had asserted the opposite of in a comment: a full page redraw took 43 ms,
**2.1 frames**, so page changes could not "outrun the beam" as claimed and the
bottom row tore. Two fixes: each key was being filled twice, a black rect then a
rounded face over 92% of it, and dropping the redundant clear took a key from
2.88 ms to 1.65 ms and the page to 1.2 frames. Then drawing each key behind the
beam removed the rest, at a cost of half a frame - 33.7 ms for a page against
24.8 unsynchronised.

Worth keeping: the estimate is now measured at boot with the backlight off
rather than carried as a constant. The old build's 8000 us was sized for
decoding a GIF frame and means nothing for drawing a rounded rectangle.
