# A 3D die on the deck, and keeping it fair

**Decision.** The dice widget's die is a cube the deck renders and simulates
itself (`firmware/src/wheel.cpp`, look version 3), not a drum of face pictures.
A tap throws it; it tumbles, hops, bounces off the key's edges, rolls and lies
flat wherever it stops. The deck reports its pose (face, place, turn) and the
app draws the same pose at rest (`src/shared/die.ts`).

**Why on the deck.** The user wanted it "rotating so fast and hitting walls,
think like it's 3D, then it stops wherever it is". Frames from the app take
20-130 ms each over USB ([[deck-first-for-animation]]). The look is 363 bytes;
the old drum die was 22 KB.

**Within its key, not across the screen.** The user wrote "roll around the grid"
and "boundary of screen". The deck's printed cover hides everything between the
key openings, so a die crossing keys would vanish under it. It stays in its key.

**Fairness - found by simulating before building.** A Node port of the physics
over thousands of throws:

- A throw that tumbles about a random axis by a random angle came up on its
  starting face about 40% of the time. A random axis-angle is not a uniformly
  random rotation; small turns are over-weighted.
- Picking where the air tumble ends as a Haar-random rotation (a random unit
  quaternion, Shoemake's method) is fair only if it is reached exactly. Landing
  3% short of the planned spin skewed it 21% / 10%. So the air phase is driven
  by elapsed time, not integrated.
- After landing, the spin must not continue about the air axis, which is tied to
  the chosen rotation. On the table it comes from the roll plus a fresh twist,
  and in the air the walls treat the die as a ball, so the path does not depend
  on how it is turned.
- With all three: 120,000 simulated throws came up 16.50-16.79% on every face.
  40 on the deck: [6, 4, 10, 7, 6, 7] (chi-squared p about 0.7).

**Speed.** The first build ran at 21 fps (26-37 ms a frame). Measuring each part
and a benchmark with no memory access showed the float code itself was slow.

- `-O2` and fast-math for `wheel.cpp` (min, max and square roots as single
  steps) took it to about 50 fps.
- Walking each face row by row, a shade table per face, pips drawn as their own
  ellipses and skipping black pixels under the shadow took it to 53-56 fps. The
  panel refreshes at about 54 Hz, so that is every frame it can show.
- An internal-RAM scratch buffer made no measurable difference and was taken
  out.

**Alternatives set aside.** App-rendered sprite frames (too big to send, and
limited to set orientations). Letting the app pick the face and steering the
settle onto it (a visible late flip). The drum of faces (not 3D, which the user
asked for).

**Would change it.** A measured bias on the deck over many throws. A panel with
headroom for a second animated die at full rate: two dice on one page share the
~54 Hz budget, untested.
